## Text

Summary: Mars paid RM 3.04 million of earnest deposits and interest towards buying land from Cubic, failed to sign the sale agreement after four extensions, and Cubic forfeited the sum. The Federal Court overturned the long-standing view in Selva Kumar (1995) that actual loss must always be proved, and held that s 75 applies to forfeiture of deposits as well as damages clauses.

Holding, as summarised in the source note: "The Federal Court decided that Section 75 allowed compensation as agreed by the parties in the liquidated damages clause, irrespective of whether actual loss or damage is proven, provided that the provision made is not extravagant, exorbitant or unconscionable and such liquidated damages claims are then enforceable. The innocent party needs only to show that there was a breach of contract. The onus then lies on the defaulting party to prove that the sum stipulated in the liquidated damages clause is unreasonable."

Para 70 of the judgment, as quoted in the practitioner blog named in licence_note: "The initial onus lies on the party seeking to enforce a clause under section 75 of the Act to adduce evidence that firstly, there was a breach of contract and that secondly, the contract contains a clause specifying a sum to be paid upon breach. Once these two elements have been established, the innocent party is entitled to receive a sum not exceeding the amount stipulated in the contract irrespective of whether actual damage or loss is proven, subject always to the defaulting party proving the unreasonableness of the damages clause including the sum stated therein, if any."

## In plain words

In Malaysia, a fixed sum for breach (liquidated damages, a forfeited deposit, a termination fee) can now be claimed without proving the actual loss. The side that broke the contract has to prove the sum is unreasonable. The court can still cut it down to reasonable compensation, and the named sum is the maximum.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

A high early-termination fee or fixed damages for breach of confidentiality is more dangerous after Cubic Electronics, because the burden of showing it is unreasonable sits on the party in breach. Negotiate the number before signing rather than counting on a court to reduce it. Applies with s 75 (my-ca1950-s75).
