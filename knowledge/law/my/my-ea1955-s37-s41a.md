## Text

Section 37(1)(a), and the definition of 'eligible period' in s 37(1)(d)(ii):

"(a) Every female employee shall be entitled—"

"(i) to maternity leave for an eligible period in respect of each confinement; and"

"(ii) “eligible period” means a period of maternity leave of not less than *ninety-eight consecutive days."

Section 41A(1) and (2):

"(1) Where a female employee is pregnant or is suffering from an illness arising out of her pregnancy, it shall be an offence for her employer to terminate her services or give her notice of termination of service, except on the grounds of—"

"(a) wilful breach of a condition of the contract of service under subsection 13(2);"

"(b) misconduct under subsection 14(1); or"

"(c) closure of the employer’s business."

"(2) Where the service of a female employee under subsection (1) is terminated, the burden of proving that such termination is not on the ground of her pregnancy or on the ground of illness arising out of her pregnancy, shall rest on the employer."

## In plain words

A female employee the Act covers is entitled to maternity leave of at least 98 consecutive days for each confinement; before the 2022 amendment the minimum was 60 days. Allowance is paid when she meets the service tests in s 37(2). While she is pregnant or ill because of the pregnancy, her employer commits an offence by ending her job or giving notice, unless the reason is wilful breach of a condition of the contract, misconduct, or closure of the business. If she is dismissed, the employer has to prove the dismissal was not about the pregnancy.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

A contract that offers fewer than 98 days of maternity leave, makes the leave unpaid for an eligible employee, or lets the employer end the job during pregnancy for any reason, is a red flag for a covered employee. Use this for the leave clause and for termination or probation clauses that could catch a pregnant employee.
