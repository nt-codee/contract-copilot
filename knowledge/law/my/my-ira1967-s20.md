## Text

Section 20 (the Act's word for the employee is 'workman'):

"20. (1) Where a workman, irrespective of whether he is a member of a trade union of workmen or otherwise, considers that he has been dismissed without just cause or excuse by his employer, he may make representations in writing to the Director General to be reinstated in his former employment; the representations may be filed at the office of the Director General nearest to the place of employment from which the workman was dismissed."

"(1A) The Director General shall not entertain any representations under subsection (1) unless such representations are filed within sixty days of the dismissal:"

"Provided that where a workman is dismissed with notice he may file a representation at any time during the period of such notice but not later than sixty days from the expiry thereof."

"(3) Where the Director General is satisfied that there is no likelihood of the representations being settled under subsection (2), the Director General shall refer the representations to the Court for an award."

"(4) Where an award has been made under subsection (3), the award shall operate as a bar to any action for damages by the workman in any court in respect of wrongful dismissal."

## In plain words

A worker who thinks they were dismissed without just cause or excuse can write to the Director General of Industrial Relations and ask to be reinstated. The letter must be filed within 60 days of the dismissal (during the notice period if the dismissal was with notice, but not later than 60 days after the notice ends). The Director General first tries to settle it, and if that fails sends it to the Industrial Court for an award. An award stops a separate court claim for damages for wrongful dismissal. Whether a particular person counts as a 'workman' depends on the Act's definition, which is not quoted here.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

Probation or 'terminate at will' wording in a contract does not by itself say whether this route is open to a worker on probation; that is a legal question these sections do not answer. Mention the 60-day deadline to anyone who has been dismissed, and treat a clause by which the employee gives up claims about dismissal as a risk to raise with the other side.
