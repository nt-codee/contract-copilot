## Text

"33. (1) In any proceedings under this Act or arising out of a hire-purchase agreement, or instituted pursuant to subsection (4), where it appears to the court that the transaction is harsh and unconscionable or is otherwise such that it will be just to give relief the court may reopen the transaction."

"(b) relieve the hirer and any guarantor from payment of any sum in excess of such sum in respect of the cash price, terms charges, and other charges as the court adjudges to be fairly and reasonably payable;"

## In plain words

A court can reopen a hire-purchase deal it finds harsh and unconscionable and cut the sums a hirer or guarantor must pay to what is fair. A hirer or guarantor who wants this relief has to start proceedings within four months of the relevant event (s 33(5)). Version warning: this is the 2013 reprint. The Hire-Purchase (Amendment) Act 2026 (in force 1 June 2026) changed how interest and early-settlement balances are worked out for new agreements, so check the current text before relying on this section.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

Heavy fees, hidden charges or punishing default sums do not become safe just because the hirer signed. This section is the backstop, but the four-month window is short.
