## Text

Section 12(1), (2) and (4), read in the Reprint:

"(1) Either party to a contract of service may at any time give to the other party notice of his intention to terminate such contract of service."

"(2) The length of such notice shall be the same for both employer and employee and shall be determined by a provision made in writing for such notice in the terms of the contract of service, or, in the absence of such provision in writing, shall not be less than—"

"(a) four weeks’ notice if the employee has been so employed for less than two years on the date on which the notice is given;"

"(b) six weeks’ notice if he has been so employed for two years or more but less than five years on such date;"

"(c) eight weeks’ notice if he has been so employed for five years or more on such date:"

"(4) Such notice shall be written and may be given at any time, and the day on which the notice is given shall be included in the period of the notice."

## In plain words

For an employee the Act covers, either side can end the job by giving notice, and the contract must set one notice length that applies to both the employer and the employee. A contract that asks the employee for three months but lets the company end the job on one week breaks that rule. If the contract says nothing in writing about notice, the minimum is 4, 6 or 8 weeks depending on years of service. Notice has to be in writing. Either side may waive the right to notice.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

Check the notice clause of any Malaysian employment contract for a mismatch between the employee's notice and the employer's notice, and for 'pay in lieu' wording that only the employer can use. The Act applies in Peninsular Malaysia and Labuan; Sabah and Sarawak have their own labour ordinances, so check where the job is based. Whether a mismatched clause is struck out, replaced by the statutory minimum or left alone is a question for a lawyer or the Labour Department. Treat the mismatch as a clear risk to raise and ask to have fixed.
