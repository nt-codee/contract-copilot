## Text

Section 24(1) and (2):

"(1) No deductions shall be made by an employer from the wages of an employee otherwise than in accordance with this Act."

"(2) It shall be lawful for an employer to make the following deductions:"

"(a) deductions to the extent of any overpayment of wages made during the immediately preceding three months from the month in which deductions are to be made, by the employer to the employee by the employer’s mistake;"

"(b) deductions for the indemnity due to the employer by the employee under subsection 13(1);"

"(c) deductions for the recovery of advances of wages made under section 22 provided no interest is charged on the advances; and"

"(d) deductions authorized by any other written law."

## In plain words

An employer cannot take money out of an employee's wages unless the Act says it may. The permitted list in s 24(2) is short: a recent overpayment made by mistake, the indemnity owed when an employee leaves without notice, wage advances (no interest), and deductions authorised by other written law. A training cost, a bond or a 'liquidated damages' amount for leaving early is not on that list. Other deductions (union dues, a provident scheme, goods bought from the employer, accommodation) need the employee's written request, and some also the Director General's permission.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

A clause that lets the employer set off 'training costs', a bond or an unrecovered bonus against salary or the final pay is a red flag for any employee the Act covers (see the First Schedule entry for who is covered). Whether the employer can still sue for a genuine, reasonable training-cost repayment through a separate claim is a different question and not answered by this section; ask for any repayment to be pro-rated, capped to real cost and never taken from wages.
