## Text

Section 47 (30 days): "any unstamped or insufficiently stamped instrument not being a cheque or promissory note drawn or made within Malaysia may be stamped after execution on payment of the unpaid duty if the instrument is presented for stamping within thirty days of its execution if executed within Malaysia"

Section 47A(1)(a): "twenty-five ringgit or five per centum of the amount of the deficient duty, whichever sum be the greater, if the instrument is stamped within 3 months after the time for stamping;"

Section 47A(1)(c): "one hundred ringgit or twenty per centum of the amount of the deficient duty, whichever sum be the greater, in any other case."

Section 52(1): "No instrument chargeable with duty shall be admitted in evidence for any purpose by any person having by law or consent of parties authority to receive evidence, or shall be acted upon, registered, or authenticated by any such person or by any public officer, unless such instrument is duly stamped:"

Section 52(1), proviso (a) (the PDF page break falls inside the sentence, so it is quoted in two parts): "any such instrument shall, subject to all just exceptions, be admitted in evidence on payment of the duty and the"

Section 52(1), proviso (a), continued: "penalty, if any, chargeable in respect thereof under section 43 or 47A"

## In plain words

A tenancy agreement should be stamped within 30 days of signing. After that it can still be stamped, but with a penalty on top of the duty: RM25 or 5% of the missing duty (whichever is more) if stamped within 3 months late, RM50 or 10% if between 3 and 6 months late, and RM100 or 20% after that (s 47A(1)(b) is the middle band and is not quoted above). Until it is stamped, section 52 says the agreement cannot be accepted in evidence or acted upon; the proviso lets it be admitted once the duty and any penalty are paid.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

This is why an unstamped tenancy matters: a landlord or tenant who later needs to enforce the agreement (for example to claim unpaid rent or recover a deposit) may first have to pay the duty and penalty. A fair agreement says who arranges and pays for stamping, and by when. A red flag is a clause that makes the tenant bear the landlord's share of stamping costs and penalties, or a landlord who keeps the only signed copy and never stamps it.
