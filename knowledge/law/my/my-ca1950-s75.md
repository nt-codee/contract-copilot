## Text

"75. When a contract has been broken, if a sum is named in the contract as the amount to be paid in case of such breach, or if the contract contains any other stipulation by way of penalty, the party complaining of the breach is entitled, whether or not actual damage or loss is proved to have been caused thereby, to receive from the party who has broken the contract reasonable compensation not exceeding the amount so named or, as the case may be, the penalty stipulated for."

"Explanation—A stipulation for increased interest from the date of default may be a stipulation by way of penalty."

## In plain words

If a contract names a sum payable on breach (liquidated damages, a termination fee, a forfeited deposit), a court awards reasonable compensation up to that sum, not automatically the full sum. The named amount is a ceiling. Since Cubic Electronics, the innocent side need not prove its actual loss; the party in breach must show the sum is unreasonable.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

Look for early-termination fees, "pay all remaining fees for the term" clauses, fixed damages for breach of confidentiality, and penalty interest on late payment. Under Malaysian law these are capped at reasonable compensation, but the burden of proving unreasonableness falls on the party in breach (my-case-cubic-electronics), so a high named sum is still a real risk.
