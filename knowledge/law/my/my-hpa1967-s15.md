## Text

"15. (1) The hirer of any goods comprised in a hire-purchase agreement may terminate the agreement by returning the goods to the owner during ordinary business hours at the place at which the owner ordinarily carries on business or to the place specified for that purpose in the agreement."

"(2) Where the nature of the goods comprised in a hire-purchase agreement or the facilities available at the place or places of business of the owner or the place specified in the agreement is or are such that it would be impracticable to return the goods to such a place, the hirer may terminate the agreement by returning the goods to any place agreed to by the parties to the agreement."

## In plain words

A hirer in Malaysia can end a hire-purchase by handing the goods back at the owner's place of business during business hours. The Act then sorts out the money: the goods are valued, and either the hirer gets any surplus or the owner can claim any shortfall (s 15(5)). An agreement cannot take this right away (see my-hpa1967-s34). Version warning: this is the 2013 reprint. The Hire-Purchase (Amendment) Act 2026 (in force 1 June 2026) changed how interest and early-settlement balances are worked out for new agreements, so check the current text before relying on this section.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

Look for a clause saying the hirer can never end the agreement early, or that 'all remaining instalments become due' if the hirer hands the goods back. A hirer who is struggling to pay may have a statutory way out that the contract does not mention.
