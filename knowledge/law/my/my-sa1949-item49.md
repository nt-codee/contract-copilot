## Text

First Schedule, item 49: "LEASE OR AGREEMENT FOR LEASE of any immovable property and for securing the payment for the provision of services or facilities or to other matters or things in connection with such lease:"

Item 49(a): "(a) Without fine or premium when the average rent and other considerations calculated for a whole year—"

Column heads (lease length): "Not exceeding one year Exceeding one but not exceeding three years Exceeding Three years or for any indefinite period"

Item 49(a)(i): "(i) Does not exceed RM2,400 Nil Nil Nil"

Item 49(a)(ii): "(ii) For every RM250 or part thereof in excess of RM2,400 1.00 2.00 4.00"

Third Schedule, item 8 (section 33: who bears the duty): "LEASE OR AGREEMENT FOR LEASE—No. 49: (a) Lease or agreement The lessee (b) Counterpart The lessor"

The PDF text layer lays the table out in rows, so the three duty figures (1.00, 2.00, 4.00) read across the three lease-length columns in the order the column heads are printed above.

## In plain words

A written tenancy agreement for land or a building is a stamped document. For a lease with no premium, the duty depends on the average yearly rent and the length of the lease. The first RM2,400 of yearly rent is not charged. Above that, the duty is RM1 (lease of up to one year), RM2 (over one year and up to three years) or RM4 (over three years or no fixed end) for every RM250 of yearly rent, or part of RM250. Worked example (this entry's arithmetic, not the Act's): a one-year lease at RM24,000 a year has RM21,600 above the free band, which is 87 blocks of RM250, so the duty is RM87; a two-year lease at the same rent is RM174. Under section 33 and the Third Schedule, the Act puts the duty on the lease on the tenant (the lessee) and the duty on the counterpart on the landlord (the lessor). The Act does not stop the parties from agreeing between themselves who ends up paying. Premium and percentage-rent leases follow items 49(b) to (d).

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

Most tenancy agreements are cheap to stamp, but a clause that makes one side pay all stamp duty, penalties and agent's costs is worth noticing. Late stamping carries a penalty (my-sa1949-s47-s52), and the duty clause should say who pays that penalty.
