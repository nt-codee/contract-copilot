## Text

"129. (1) (Deleted by Act A1727).

(2) A data controller may transfer any personal data of a data subject to any place outside Malaysia if— (a) there is in that place in force any law which is substantially similar to this Act; or (b) that place ensures an adequate level of protection in relation to the processing of personal data which is at least equivalent to the level of protection afforded by this Act.

(3) Notwithstanding subsection (2), a data controller may transfer any personal data to a place outside Malaysia if— (a) the data subject has given his consent to the transfer; (b) the transfer is necessary for the performance of a contract between the data subject and the data controller; …"

## In plain words

A Malaysian business may send personal data abroad if the destination has a law substantially similar to Malaysia's PDPA or gives at least equivalent protection, or if another listed ground applies, such as the person's consent or necessity for a contract with that person.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

Cloud and SaaS vendors often store or access data outside Malaysia. The contract should say where data will be stored and accessed, require the vendor to tell you before moving it, and commit it to protection at least equivalent to the PDPA, so you can show s 129 is satisfied.
