## Text

"34. Any provision in any hire-purchase agreement whereby— (a) any right conferred on the hirer by this Act to determine a hire-purchase agreement is excluded or restricted; (b) the hirer is subject to any greater liability on the determination, in any manner whatsoever, of the hire-purchase agreement or of the bailment thereunder, than the liability to which he would be subject if the hire-purchase agreement were determined in accordance with this Act;"

"(c) the hirer is required to pay interest on any overdue instalments at a rate exceeding eight per centum per annum under hire-purchase agreement which provides for terms charges at a fixed rate or, two per centum above the prevailing rate of terms charges, under a hire-purchase agreement which provides for terms charges at a variable rate, simple interest calculated on a daily basis or such other rate of interest as may be prescribed;"

"(e) the owner or any person acting on his behalf is authorized to enter upon any premises for the purpose of taking possession of goods otherwise than in accordance with the provisions of this Act comprised in the hire-purchase agreement or is relieved from liability for any such entry;"

## In plain words

Section 34 lists contract terms that do not work against a hirer. Among them: terms that cut off the hirer's right to end the hiring, terms that make the hirer pay more on ending than the Act allows, a late-payment rate above 8% a year simple interest (fixed-rate deals, 2013 text), and terms that let the owner enter premises to take the goods other than as the Act allows. The wording on the late-payment rate and on 'terms charges' may have changed with the 2026 amendment. Version warning: this is the 2013 reprint. The Hire-Purchase (Amendment) Act 2026 (in force 1 June 2026) changed how interest and early-settlement balances are worked out for new agreements, so check the current text before relying on this section.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

Use this as a checklist: no early-settlement penalty beyond the Act, no compounding or inflated default interest, no self-help repossession. The text of s 34 continues beyond (e); items (d), (f) and (g) are not quoted here.
