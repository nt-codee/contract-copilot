## Text

"74. (1) When a contract has been broken, the party who suffers by the breach is entitled to receive, from the party who has broken the contract, compensation for any loss or damage caused to him thereby, which naturally arose in the usual course of things from the breach, or which the parties knew, when they made the contract, to be likely to result from the breach of it.

(2) Such compensation is not to be given for any remote and indirect loss or damage sustained by reason of the breach."

## In plain words

If a contract is broken, the innocent side can claim the losses that would normally flow from that breach, plus losses both sides knew were likely when they signed. Remote and indirect losses are not recoverable at all.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

This is the default position that liability caps and exclusions change. Because remote and indirect loss is already excluded by law, a vendor clause excluding "indirect or consequential loss" mainly confirms the default; the real question is whether the cap or exclusion also cuts out ordinary, direct losses. If you will suffer a specific, unusual loss from a failure, tell the vendor in writing before signing so it is within what "the parties knew".
