## Text

Section 2: "This Act applies only to Peninsular Malaysia and the Federal Territory of Labuan."

Section 213(1)(a): "In this Act, "tenancy exempt from registration" means— (a) any tenancy or subtenancy for a term not exceeding three years granted pursuant to section 223;"

Section 213(2)(a): "any such transaction shall be valid whether effected in writing or by word of mouth only;"

Section 213(3) (opening words): "no tenancy exempt from registration granted after the commencement of this Act shall be binding on—"

## In plain words

In Peninsular Malaysia and Labuan, a tenancy of up to three years does not need to be registered at the land office and can be made orally or in any written form. The catch in s 213(3) is that an unregistered short tenancy does not bind someone who later buys the property or takes a lease or charge over it from the landlord, unless the tenancy has been protected by an endorsement on the land's title (s 213(3) refers to Chapter 7 of Part Eighteen of the Code for how this is done). Sabah and Sarawak have their own land laws, which this entry does not cover.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

For a residential or shop-lot tenancy of three years or less, the absence of land-office registration is normal, not a red flag. But the tenant's protection if the landlord sells or mortgages the property is weaker than for a registered lease, so a longer tenancy or a large deposit and fit-out spend may justify asking a lawyer whether the tenancy should be protected by an endorsement on the title.
