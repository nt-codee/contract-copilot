## Text

Section 221(2): "Every lease granted pursuant to this section shall be for a term exceeding three years."

Section 223(1) (opening words): "tenancies (or, as the case may be, subtenancies) for terms not exceeding three years may be granted as follows:"

Section 224(a): "if the term is from week to week, month to month, year to year, or on any other periodic basis, it shall be treated as a term for a period equal to its minimum possible duration"

Section 224(b): "if the term is for a fixed period, no account shall be taken of the fact that it is capable of renewal in pursuance of an option, or is liable to be determined before the expiry of that period by the giving of notice or the happening of any other event."

Section 224 says it applies 'for the purposes of this Chapter', which is the Chapter on powers of leasing in Part Fifteen of the Code.

## In plain words

Under the Code, a 'lease' is for a term of more than three years (a registered instrument on the prescribed form), while a 'tenancy' is for three years or less. When working out which side of the line an agreement falls on, a fixed term is counted without any renewal option, and a rolling month-to-month or year-to-year term is counted at its shortest possible length. Under paragraph (b), a renewal option is ignored when counting a fixed term. The section does not say how a clause that renews automatically unless notice is given should be counted, so this entry does not draw a conclusion on that point.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

Check the headline term and the renewal wording together. A 2-year tenancy that renews automatically for another 2 years can add up to more than three years in practice. Ask whether the renewal is the tenant's option or automatic, and whether the parties intend a registered lease on the prescribed form. This is a registration point only; it does not make an automatic renewal clause unfair or unlawful by itself. General information, not legal advice.
