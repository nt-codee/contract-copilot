## Text

Annual leave, s 60E(1):

"(1) An employee shall be entitled to paid annual leave of—"

"(a) eight days for every twelve months of continuous service with the same employer if he has been employed by that employer for a period of less than two years;"

"(b) twelve days for every twelve months of continuous service with the same employer if he has been employed by that employer for a period of two years or more but less than five years; and"

"(c) sixteen days for every twelve months of continuous service with the same employer if he has been employed by that employer for a period of five years or more,"

Sick leave, s 60F(1)(aa) (no hospitalisation):

"(i) of fourteen days in the aggregate in each calendar year if the employee has been employed for less than two years;"

"(ii) of eighteen days in the aggregate in each calendar year if the employee has been employed for two years or more but less than five years;"

"(iii) of twenty-two days in the aggregate in each calendar year if the employee has been employed for five years or more; or"

Paternity leave, s 60FA(1) and (3):

"(1) Subject to subsection (3), a married male employee shall be entitled to a paid paternity leave at his ordinary rate of pay for a period of seven consecutive days in respect of each confinement."

"(a) he has been employed by the same employer at least twelve months immediately before the commencement of such paternity leave; and"

"(b) he has notified his employer of the pregnancy of his spouse at least thirty days from the expected confinement or as early as possible after the birth."

## In plain words

For an employee the Act covers, the legal floor is 8, 12 or 16 days of paid annual leave a year (by years of service), 14, 18 or 22 days of paid sick leave a year with a medical certificate, and, for a married male employee with 12 months' service who tells the employer of his spouse's pregnancy, 7 consecutive days of paid paternity leave per confinement. A contract can give more, but not less. Annual leave is in addition to rest days and paid public holidays.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

Compare the leave clause to these numbers. A contract that gives fewer days than the statutory floor to a covered employee is a red flag; 'subject to the employer's discretion' wording on sick or annual leave is a risk to raise.
