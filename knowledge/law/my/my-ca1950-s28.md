## Text

"28. Every agreement by which anyone is restrained from exercising a lawful profession, trade, or business of any kind, is to that extent void."

"Exception 1—One who sells the goodwill of a business may agree with the buyer to refrain carrying on a similar business, within specified local limits, so long as the buyer, or any person deriving title to the goodwill from him, carries on a like business therein: Provided that such limits appear to the court reasonable, regard being had to the nature of the business."

(Exceptions 2 and 3 cover partners on or before dissolution of a partnership, and during the partnership.)

## In plain words

In Malaysia, a promise not to carry on a lawful trade or business is void to the extent it restrains that trade. Unlike Singapore or England, there is no general "it was reasonable" escape: the only exceptions are sale of a business's goodwill and partnerships. Only the restraining part fails; the rest of the contract can survive.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

Non-compete wording inside an NDA or a vendor agreement under Malaysian law is likely void, but it still deters and costs money to fight, and a foreign governing-law clause may change the answer. Whether a non-solicitation clause counts as a restraint under s 28 is not settled by this text; treat it as a risk to raise rather than assume either way. Watch for a proposed amendment that would introduce a reasonableness test.
