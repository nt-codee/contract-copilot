## Text

Summary: the bank failed to make a progress payment under a housing loan, the purchase fell through, and the borrowers sued. The bank relied on a clause excluding loss of income, profit, savings and indirect or consequential damages. The High Court upheld the clause; the Court of Appeal and then the Federal Court did not.

Holding, as summarised in the source note: "in a unanimous 5 panel decision, the Federal Court upheld the Court of Appeal’s decision and affirmed that where an exclusion clause in an agreement sought to (1) exonerate a contract breaker of its liability for a breach of that contract and (2) negates the contract breakers’ liability to pay compensation for non-performance of that contract, it would be void. Section 29 of the Contracts Act, 1950 may be invoked to strike down and invalidate such a clause."

## In plain words

Under Malaysian law, a clause that would leave the innocent party with no remedy at all for a proven breach can be struck down as void under s 29. The question is whether, on the facts, the clause absolutely blocks the claim. The case does not say that every cap or exclusion is invalid.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

Vendor terms that exclude "all liability" or every realistic head of loss for the vendor's own breach are open to challenge in Malaysia. A mutual cap that still leaves a meaningful remedy is a different question that this case did not decide. Use the case to argue for a real, mutual cap rather than a total exclusion.
