## Text

"40. When a party to a contract has refused to perform, or disabled himself from performing, his promise in its entirety, the promisee may put an end to the contract, unless he has signified, by words or conduct, his acquiescence in its continuance."

## In plain words

If the other side refuses to do what it promised, or makes itself unable to, you may end the contract, unless you have shown by words or conduct that you are happy for it to carry on.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

This is the general-law right to terminate that sits behind any termination clause. If a vendor stops delivering altogether, the customer can end the contract even if the termination clause is narrow, but continuing to accept and pay for partial service may be read as accepting the contract's continuance. Contract termination rights (notice, cure periods) usually add to this right.
