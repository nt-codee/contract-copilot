## Text

Section 8 (opening words): "Harta yang boleh disita di bawah suatu waran distress hendaklah tidak termasuk"

Section 8(c): "pakaian yang perlu oleh penyewa dan tempat tidur yang perlu bagi diri dan keluarganya;"

Section 8(d): "barang-barang dalam milikan penyewa itu bagi maksud dibawa, ditempa, diusahakan, atau selainnya diuruskan dalam menjalankan tred atau perniagaan biasanya;"

Unofficial English gloss: property that may be seized under a warrant of distress does not include (c) the clothing the tenant needs and the bedding needed for the tenant and family, and (d) goods in the tenant's possession for carrying, working on or otherwise handling in the ordinary course of the tenant's trade or business. The section lists other exclusions (a), (b), (e), (f), (g) that are not quoted here.

## In plain words

Even with a court warrant, a landlord cannot seize everything. The tenant's necessary clothing and bedding are protected, and so are goods the tenant holds to handle in its ordinary trade (the gloss above is an approximation, not an official translation).

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

For a shop-lot or room rental, a clause giving the landlord the right to take 'all goods on the premises' as security for arrears is wider than the Act allows even when the court is involved. If the clause also lets the landlord act without a warrant, section 4 (my-da1951-s4-s5) is the bigger problem. General information, not legal advice.
