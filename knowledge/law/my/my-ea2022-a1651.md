## Text

This entry records the amending Act rather than a section. What the fetched sources say:

- Cover of the Reprint: "Latest amendment made by Act A1651 which came into operation on … … 1 January 2023"

- Note printed in the Reprint: "*NOTE—see paragraph 46(1), (2) and (3) of the Employment (Amendment) Act 2022 [Act A1651] which comes into operation on 1 January 2023"

- Gazette notice P.U.(B) 368 (dated 12 August 2022): "the Minister appoints 1 September 2022 as the date on which the Act comes into operation."

- Reprint note to s 37 on the maternity change: "Previously “sixty”—see paragraph 12(b) of the Employment (Amendment) Act 2022 [Act A1651]."

## In plain words

The 2022 amendment is the reason many older Malaysian employment contracts look out of date. It cut the normal working week from 48 to 45 hours (s 60A), raised the maternity leave minimum from 60 to 98 days (s 37), added 7 days of paid paternity leave (s 60FA), added protection against ending a pregnant employee's job (s 41A), added the employee-status presumption (s 101C), raised several maximum fines from RM10,000 to RM50,000, and allowed flexible working arrangements to be set by regulations. A matching Order widened the First Schedule so the Act covers every employee, with the RM4,000 line limiting only certain provisions. All of this took effect on 1 January 2023 according to the Reprint.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

A contract signed before 2023 or copied from an older template may still say 48 hours, 60 days of maternity leave, or no paternity leave. Treat that as out of date, and a request for the contract to be updated is a reasonable ask. The Employment Act changes only protect employees the Act covers in Peninsular Malaysia and Labuan.
