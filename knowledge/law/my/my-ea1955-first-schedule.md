## Text

First Schedule, as printed in the Reprint (a table of employees and the provisions of the Act not applicable to them):

"1. Any person who has entered into a contract of service."

"1a. Notwithstanding paragraph 1, the person whose wages exceeds four thousand ringgit a month."

Provisions listed beside paragraph 1a as not applicable: "Subsections 60(3), 60a(3), 60c(2a), 60d(3) and 60d(4) and section 60j"

"2. Any person who, irrespective of the amount of wages he earns in a month, has entered into a contract of service with an employer in pursuance of which—"

"(1) he is engaged in manual labour including such labour as an artisan or apprentice:"

"3. For the purpose of this Schedule “wages” means wages as defined in section 2, but shall not include any payment by way of commissions, subsistence allowance and overtime payment."

The Order that amended the start date (P.U.(A) 273/2022, para 2) says: "The Employment (Amendment of First Schedule) Order 2022 [P.U. (A) 262/2022] is amended in subparagraph 1(2) by substituting for the words “1 September 2022” the words “1 January 2023”."

## In plain words

Since 1 January 2023 the Act's protections reach every employee under a contract of service in Peninsular Malaysia and Labuan, whatever the wage. Before that, people earning over RM2,000 a month were mostly outside it. Today an employee earning more than RM4,000 a month is left out only of a short list of provisions (overtime, and the pay rates for rest days, public holidays and shift work), unless the job is manual labour, driving or operating vehicles, supervising manual workers, working on a vessel, or domestic work, where coverage holds at any wage. 'Wages' for this test do not count commission, subsistence allowance or overtime pay.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

Before applying an overtime, leave or notice rule to a Malaysian employee, check monthly wages against RM4,000 and the job type. Notice (s 12), deductions (s 24), annual and sick leave and maternity rules apply to covered employees whether they earn above or below RM4,000 (apart from the carve-outs for domestic employees). Freelancers and contractors are outside the Act only if they are not employees; see the s 101C entry on how that is judged.
