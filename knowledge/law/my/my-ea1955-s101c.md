## Text

Section 101C(1):

"(1) In any proceeding for an offence under this Act, in the absence of a written contract of service relating to any category of employee under the First Schedule, it shall be presumed until the contrary is proved that a person is an employee—"

"(a) where his manner of work is subject to the control or direction of another person;"

"(b) where his hours of work are subject to the control or direction of another person;"

"(c) where he is provided with tools, materials or equipments by another person to execute work;"

"(d) where his work constitutes an integral part of another person’s business;"

"(e) where his work is performed solely for the benefit of another person; or"

"(f) where payment is made to him in return for work done by him at regular intervals and such payment constitutes the majority of his income."

## In plain words

If someone is called a freelancer or contractor but in practice the other side controls how and when they work, supplies the tools, treats the work as a core part of its own business, takes all of the benefit of the work, or pays them regularly and that pay is most of their income, a prosecution under the Employment Act starts from the presumption that they are an employee, until shown otherwise. The label in the contract does not decide it.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

Check any 'freelance' or 'independent contractor' agreement that also sets hours, requires working only for one client, or provides equipment and a fixed monthly payment. Those facts point towards employment, with notice, leave, overtime and deduction rules attached. This presumption works in prosecutions only, and only where there is no written contract of service, so what it means for a civil claim is a question for a lawyer.
