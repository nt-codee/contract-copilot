## Text

Section 4 (heading: 'Tiada distress selain di bawah Akta ini'): "Tiada tuan tanah boleh menahan harta untuk mendapat sewa kecuali dalam cara yang diperuntukkan oleh Akta ini."

Section 5(1): "Seorang tuan tanah atau ejennya yang diberi kuasa secara bertulis dengan sewajarnya boleh memohon ex-parte kepada seorang Hakim atau Pendaftar bagi suatu perintah untuk mengeluarkan suatu waran, yang akan dipanggil suatu waran distress, bagi mendapat kembali sewa yang kena dibayar atau harus dibayar kepada tuan tanah itu oleh seorang penyewa mana-mana premis bagi suatu tempoh tidak lebih daripada dua belas bulan genap penyewaan sejurus sebelum tarikh permohonan itu"

Section 7 (opening words): "Suatu waran distress hendaklah dialamatkan kepada bailif, mengarahkannya seterusnya untuk menahan apa-apa harta alih yang dijumpai olehnya di atas premis yang dinamakan dalamnya"

Unofficial English gloss: s 4: no landlord may seize ('distrain') property for rent except in the way this Act provides. s 5(1): a landlord, or an agent authorised in writing, may apply without notice to a Judge or Registrar for an order to issue a 'warrant of distress' to recover rent due for up to twelve complete months of the tenancy before the application. s 7: the warrant is addressed to a bailiff, who is directed to seize movable property found on the named premises.

## In plain words

Under this Act a landlord cannot simply take or lock up a tenant's belongings to make the tenant pay overdue rent. Seizing goods for rent must go through a court: the landlord applies for a warrant of distress, and the court's bailiff carries it out. The warrant covers at most twelve complete months of unpaid rent. The Act is about seizing goods for rent; it does not by itself deal with taking back possession of the premises.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

Red flag for a tenancy: a clause that lets the landlord remove, hold or sell the tenant's goods after a short arrears period such as 7 days, without any court order. Section 4 says a landlord may not detain a tenant's property to get rent except in the way the Act provides, which is through a court warrant. A clause that lets the landlord re-enter and change the locks raises a separate question about possession; it is relevant to this Act when the tenant's goods are kept inside as pressure for rent. A balanced agreement lets the landlord claim arrears and interest and, where the law allows, apply to court; it does not give a self-help right of seizure. This is general information, not advice on any one dispute.
