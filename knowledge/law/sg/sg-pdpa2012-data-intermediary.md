## Text

"“data intermediary” means an organisation which processes personal data on behalf of another organisation but does not include an employee of that other organisation;"

"4.— … (2) Parts 3, 4, 5, 6 (except sections 24 and 25), 6A (except sections 26C(3)(a) and 26E) and 6B do not impose any obligation on a data intermediary in respect of its processing of personal data on behalf of and for the purposes of another organisation pursuant to a contract which is evidenced or made in writing.

(3) An organisation has the same obligation under this Act in respect of personal data processed on its behalf and for its purposes by a data intermediary as if the personal data were processed by the organisation itself."

## In plain words

A vendor that processes personal data for your business under a written contract is a "data intermediary". It has only a few direct duties: protect the data (s 24), do not keep it longer than needed (s 25), and report breaches to you (s 26C(3)(a)). Your business keeps every other PDPA obligation as if it did the processing itself.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

Because you remain responsible for what your vendor does with personal data, the service agreement is where you push your other duties onto it: process only on your instructions, limit use and disclosure, help with access and correction requests, and control overseas transfers. The written contract is also what puts the vendor in the lighter data-intermediary regime.
