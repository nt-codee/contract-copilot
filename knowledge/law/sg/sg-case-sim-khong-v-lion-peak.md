## Text

Summary: the lease made the tenant 'in default' if rent was unpaid seven days after the due date, and gave the landlord a right of re-entry. The landlord applied to the High Court for vacant possession on several grounds (rent arrears, unpaid utilities, unmaintained lifts, insolvency). The Court granted possession but, because the landlord relied on more than rent arrears, was not bound by the four-week minimum in s 18A(3) and gave two weeks. The judge said that for a rent-only default the s 18A protections (a court order, at least four weeks, and payment into court to avoid giving up possession) apply.

The lease clause (judgment at [7]): "fails to pay the Rent or any other sum payable under this lease within seven days after the due date (whether or not formally demanded)"

At [27]: "For the default under cl 6.1.1(a), ie, failure to pay rent, the applicant’s right of re-entry was subject to s 18A CLPA. Pursuant to s 18A(3) CLPA, the court order must provide for at least four weeks between the date of the order and the date of delivery of possession, during which the lessee may avoid delivering possession by paying into court the rental arrears and costs of the action."

At [3]: "I allowed the application save that I gave the respondent two weeks to deliver vacant possession of the Property."

## In plain words

A seven-day rent default clause in a lease is a trigger written into the contract, but the landlord still had to go to court for an order for possession. For a default that is only about unpaid rent, Singapore's statute (as described by the judge at [27]) requires the court order to give the tenant at least four weeks, during which the tenant can keep the premises by paying the arrears and costs into court. The judge's description of s 18A is quoted from the judgment; the Act itself was not opened.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

This is a direct comparison for a shop-lot clause that lets the landlord re-enter and change the locks after 7 days of arrears. The case shows a 7-day default clause in a real lease, but possession was obtained through a court application, and for a rent-only default the judge describes a statutory chance for the tenant to pay and stay. A lease clause that lets the landlord skip that process may overstate what the landlord can lawfully do on its own. General information, not legal advice.
