## Text

In the IRAS table, 'AAR' means the average annual rent, defined by IRAS as the higher of the average annual contractual rent or annualised market rent, including other considerations such as service charges, furniture charges and maintenance charges (excluding GST). IRAS shows leases where the AAR does not exceed $1,000 as 'Exempted' and says lease duty is rounded down to the nearest dollar, with a minimum of $1.

IRAS, 'Renting a property' (summary line): "Stamp duty on leases is payable based on the contractual rental or the market rental, whichever is higher, at the lease duty rates."

IRAS rates table, lease of 4 years or less: "0.4% of total rent for the period of the lease"

IRAS rates table, lease of more than 4 years or any indefinite term: "0.4% of 4 times the AAR for the period of the lease"

IRAS rates table, small leases: "AAR does not exceed $1,000"

IRAS, same page: "The Stamp Certificate proves that your tenancy agreement has been duly stamped."

## In plain words

A written tenancy in Singapore is stamped with lease duty. For a lease of 4 years or less the duty is 0.4% of the total rent over the lease, so a 2-year lease at $3,000 a month ($72,000) is $288 (this entry's arithmetic, not IRAS's example). IRAS says the stamp certificate proves the tenancy agreement is duly stamped.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

A fair agreement states who pays the stamp duty and who arranges stamping, and the stamp certificate is kept with the agreement. A clause that leaves the tenant paying duty and any late-stamping costs without saying so is worth a question. General information, not legal advice.
