## Text

Summary: a landlord applied for a writ of distress for eight months of unpaid rent plus contractual interest. The Assistant Registrar explained that the Distress Act 1934 lets a court issue a writ only for 'rent', that the tenancy agreement did not deem interest to be rent, and granted the writ for the unpaid rent only. The landlord was awarded indemnity costs under the tenancy's indemnity clause.

At [8]: "Distress in Singapore is thus not a self-help remedy; it can be obtained only with judicial intervention."

At [11]: "These provisions make clear that writs of distress can only be issued for the recovery of “rent”, and not also for the recovery of other sums that may be due and payable to the landlord by the tenant."

## In plain words

In Singapore a landlord cannot just walk in and take the tenant's goods for rent. The landlord must apply to the court, and the court's officer carries out the seizure. What can be recovered this way is rent, not late interest, repair costs or other charges, unless the agreement deems them to be rent.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

Compare with Malaysian law, which also requires a court warrant (my-da1951-s4-s5). For either country, a clause that gives the landlord a right to seize or sell the tenant's goods without a court is a red flag. This decision also suggests that interest and other charges are not recovered by distress unless the agreement deems them to be rent; they are normally claimed in an ordinary court claim.
