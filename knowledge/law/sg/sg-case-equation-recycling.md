## Text

Summary: a company paid a security deposit under an agreement to use part of another company's rented land. The landlord-side company forfeited the whole deposit after breaches. The High Court held (a) the law of penalties can in principle apply to a clause forfeiting a security deposit paid under a lease, unlike the 'earnest' deposits in Li Jialin (sg-case-li-jialin), (b) the particular clause was not a penalty, but (c) on its true meaning the clause let the landlord side forfeit only to cover losses actually caused by the breaches, and it had proved none, so it had to return the deposit (less some utility charges).

At [6]: "while I agree with the DJ that the law of penalties may apply to a clause providing for the forfeiture of a security deposit paid under a lease, I find that the clause in this specific case does not amount to an unenforceable penalty"

At [188(b)]: "it only has the right to forfeit and deduct from the Security Deposit to compensate himself for the losses that he has suffered by virtue of the respondent’s breach"

## In plain words

In Singapore, a security deposit that is there to secure performance (such as rent and repairs under a tenancy) is not the same as an 'earnest' deposit that binds a purchase. This High Court decision says a clause that lets the landlord keep the whole security deposit can be tested under the penalty rule, and that, as read in that case, the landlord could keep only what it could show it had lost. It is one first-instance decision on a land-use agreement, not a ruling on residential tenancies.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

A clause that says the whole deposit is forfeited for any breach, or is 'non-refundable at the landlord's discretion', is the pattern to look at. After this decision there is a reasoned argument in Singapore that such a clause is tested against the landlord's real loss. For Malaysia, the equivalent starting point is Contracts Act s 75 (my-ca1950-s75). Whether any clause is enforceable depends on its wording and facts, and this is general information, not advice.
