## Text

"3. If a contract contains a term which would exclude or restrict —
(a) any liability to which a party to a contract may be subject by reason of any misrepresentation made by him before the contract was made; or
(b) any remedy available to another party to the contract by reason of such a misrepresentation,
that term shall be of no effect except in so far as it satisfies the requirement of reasonableness as stated in section 11(1) of the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977, and it is for those claiming that the term satisfies that requirement to show that it does."

## In plain words

If a contract tries to exclude liability for false statements made before signing, that term only works if it is reasonable, and the party relying on it must prove that. This applies to all contracts, not only standard terms.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

Vendor terms often say "the customer has not relied on any statement not set out in this agreement" alongside an entire-agreement clause. In Singapore, a term that excludes liability for misrepresentation has to pass s 3; an entire-agreement clause on its own may not be enough (see sg-case-lee-chee-wei). If a sales claim matters to you, write it into the contract.
