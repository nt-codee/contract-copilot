## Text

Summary: a landlord gave notice to end a two-year office lease after one year so that it could renovate, then withdrew the notice. The tenant had already moved. In assessing the tenant's damages for the landlord's breach, the Court of Appeal asked what would put the tenant in the position it would have been in had the lease been performed (relocation costs rather than wasted fit-out costs), and applied the duty to mitigate (it refused the cost of renting premises about 50% bigger than the original).

At [17]: "the aggrieved party must take all reasonable steps to mitigate the loss consequent on the defaulting party’s breach, and cannot recover damages for any loss which it could have avoided but failed to avoid due to its own unreasonable action or inaction"

At [28]: "an award of relocation costs would, as opposed to wasted costs, better achieve the law’s aim of placing the Appellant in the position he would have been in had the Lease Agreement been performed."

## In plain words

If a landlord ends a fixed-term lease early in breach of the lease, the tenant can claim the loss caused, such as the cost of moving, but must take reasonable steps to keep that loss down.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

Use this when a landlord's early-termination or 'renovation' right looks one-sided. It shows that a tenant can be compensated for a landlord's wrongful early termination, but the recoverable amount is limited to real, reasonably incurred loss. It does not say whether a lease clause giving the landlord a right to terminate early is fair; that depends on its terms. General information, not legal advice.
