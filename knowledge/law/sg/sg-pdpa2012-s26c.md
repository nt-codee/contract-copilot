## Text

"26C.— … (2) Subject to subsection (3), where an organisation has reason to believe that a data breach affecting personal data in its possession or under its control has occurred, the organisation must conduct, in a reasonable and expeditious manner, an assessment of whether the data breach is a notifiable data breach.

(3) Where a data intermediary (other than a data intermediary mentioned in section 26E) has reason to believe that a data breach has occurred in relation to personal data that the data intermediary is processing on behalf of and for the purposes of another organisation —
(a) the data intermediary must, without undue delay, notify that other organisation of the occurrence of the data breach; and
(b) that other organisation must, upon notification by the data intermediary, conduct an assessment of whether the data breach is a notifiable data breach."

## In plain words

When a vendor processing your personal data suspects a breach, the law requires it to tell you without undue delay. Your business must then assess, reasonably and quickly, whether the breach is serious enough to be notifiable.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

"Without undue delay" is the legal floor. Because your own clock to notify the PDPC starts after your assessment (sg-pdpa2012-s26d), ask for a specific vendor notice period (for example 24 to 48 hours), the details the vendor must provide, and its cooperation with your assessment.
