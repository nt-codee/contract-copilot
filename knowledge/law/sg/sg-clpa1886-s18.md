## Text

Section 18(1), as quoted at [23] of the judgment: "Restrictions on and relief against forfeiture of leases 18.—(1) A right of re-entry or forfeiture under any provision or stipulation in a lease, for a breach of any covenant or condition in a lease, shall not be enforceable, by action or otherwise, unless the lessor serves on the lessee a notice specifying the particular breach complained of and, if the breach is capable of remedy, requiring the lessee to remedy the breach, and, in any case, requiring the lessee to make compensation in money for the breach, and the lessee fails, within a reasonable time thereafter, to remedy the breach, if it is capable of remedy, and to make reasonable compensation in money, to the satisfaction of the lessor, for the breach."

## In plain words

Even if a Singapore lease says the landlord may re-enter or end the lease on a breach, the landlord generally cannot rely on that right unless it first serves a notice that names the particular breach, asks the tenant to fix it if it can be fixed, and asks for money compensation. The tenant then has a reasonable time to respond. Non-payment of rent is dealt with separately (see sg-case-sim-khong-v-lion-peak for s 18A).

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

Section 18(1) covers a landlord's re-entry or forfeiture for breaches such as not repairing or using the premises wrongly. A clause that lets the landlord re-enter at once for such a breach, with no notice and no time to put it right, promises more than the statute allows. Rent arrears are covered by a separate section, s 18A (see sg-case-sim-khong-v-lion-peak). This is general information, not legal advice.
