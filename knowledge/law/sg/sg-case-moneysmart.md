## Text

Summary: a former employee was bound by a non-compete covering South-East Asia and other countries where the employer operated, for 12 months, with fall-back periods of 6 and 3 months if a court found the 12 months unenforceable. The employer obtained interim injunctions without notice; the court discharged them, holding the clause was not valid and enforceable.

At [49]: "it is crucial that there is a close connection between the geographical scope of the restriction and the work done by the employee prior to leaving."

At [52]: "the Non-Compete Clause is evidently too wide, and therefore unreasonable as between the parties."

At [101]: "the Non-Compete Clause neither protects a legitimate proprietary interest of the claimant nor is reasonable and fair."

## In plain words

A non-compete is judged against the employee's actual job. This employee worked on a product sold only in Singapore, but the clause blocked work for any competitor across South-East Asia, covering countries where the employer had little or no operation, only plans. The court called that too wide and refused to enforce it, even though the clause had three tiers of shorter fall-back periods.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

Compare a regional non-compete such as 'anywhere in Southeast Asia' with where the employee will actually work and which customers they will deal with. A region-wide ban for a local role, or a ban on any competing business whatever the employee's duties, is a clear red flag in a Singapore-law contract. This was an interim-stage decision on the facts of one case; it is not a rule that every regional restraint fails.
