## Text

"3.—(1) This section applies as between contracting parties where one of them deals as consumer or on the other’s written standard terms of business.

(2) As against that party, the other cannot by reference to any contract term —
(a) when himself in breach of contract, exclude or restrict any liability of his in respect of the breach; or
(b) claim to be entitled —
(i) to render a contractual performance substantially different from that which was reasonably expected of him; or
(ii) in respect of the whole or any part of his contractual obligation, to render no performance at all,
except in so far as (in any of the cases mentioned in this subsection) the contract term satisfies the requirement of reasonableness."

## In plain words

When a business signs up on a vendor's own written standard terms, the vendor can only rely on a clause that limits its liability for its own breach, or lets it deliver something substantially different or nothing at all, if that clause is reasonable.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

This is the section that protects an SME signing a vendor's click-through or standard service terms. Clauses such as "vendor may change or discontinue the service at any time" or "vendor's liability for breach is limited to one month's fees" must pass the reasonableness test. A heavily negotiated, bespoke contract may fall outside s 3.
