## Text

Summary: an employee who was investigated, suspended and then terminated immediately with pay in lieu of notice sued for breach of his employment contract and in tort. The Appellate Division dismissed his appeal and commented on the limits of the claim and on the implied duty of mutual trust and confidence.

At [21]: "It is well-established that employers may terminate employees without cause, in accordance with an express right of termination, so long as sufficient written notice or pay in lieu of notice is given."

At [69]: "it is not settled by the Court of Appeal that this implied term forms a part of Singapore law."

## In plain words

Where the contract gives a right to end employment on notice or pay in lieu, Singapore courts will usually uphold termination without cause if that notice or pay is given, and the damages an employee can claim are generally limited to the notice they should have received. The court also noted that it is not settled that every Singapore employment contract carries an implied duty of mutual trust and confidence, so an employee should not rely on that duty to fix a harsh contract.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

The contract's own notice and termination clauses carry most of the weight in Singapore. On this approach the contract's own notice terms largely decide what an employee can recover, so a lopsided clause (for example 1 week for the employer and 3 months for the employee) should be raised before signing, not after.
