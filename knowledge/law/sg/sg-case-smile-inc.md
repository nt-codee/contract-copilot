## Text

Summary: an associate dentist's contract had a non-solicitation clause, a radial (distance) clause and a non-dealing clause (together the 'Restrictive Covenants'), none with a time limit. The Court of Appeal dismissed the clinic's appeal against the High Court's dismissal of its claim.

At [30]: "The absence of a time limit alone in this particular case renders the Restrictive Covenants unreasonable as between the parties, and therefore, void and unenforceable."

At [33]: "we are not required to express a concluded view on the applicability of the doctrine of notional severance in Singapore"

## In plain words

In Singapore a restraint on an employee after they leave is tested for reasonableness, and a clause with no time limit at all failed on that point alone. The court could remove a whole unreasonable clause or strike out words, but it did not rewrite the clause to add a sensible time limit, and the clinic's lawyer accepted that striking out words would not rescue these covenants. The Court of Appeal left open whether Singapore would adopt a rule letting courts rewrite restraints; a later High Court judgment (3D Networks, in this knowledge base) says that approach has not been accepted.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

Look for a non-compete or non-solicit clause with no stated end date, or vague wording like 'for a reasonable period', in a Singapore-law employment contract. It is weak, but still worth raising since an employee may not want to test it. The result turns on facts and on the exact wording, so this is general information and not a prediction for one contract.
