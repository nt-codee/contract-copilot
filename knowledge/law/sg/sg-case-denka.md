## Text

Summary: Seraya claimed liquidated damages (LD) under electricity retailer agreements (ERAs) with Denka, in a dispute that also turned on whether Denka had breached them. The Court of Appeal declined to follow the UK Supreme Court's restatement in Cavendish v Makdessi and the Australian approach in Andrews.

At [185]: "(a) First, the Penalty Rule applies only in the context of a breach of contract. This does not preclude the applicability of other doctrines (including the narrow doctrine of unconscionability in BOM v BOK ([98] supra)) from operating where relevant.
(b) Secondly, the legal criteria to ascertain whether the Penalty Rule applies may be found in the statement of principles enunciated by Lord Dunedin in Dunlop ([1] supra). The focus is whether the clause concerned provided a genuine pre-estimate of the likely loss at the time of contracting. In this regard, the only “legitimate interest” which the Penalty Rule is concerned with is that of compensation."

At [309]: "In sum, we are satisfied that the LD clauses are not penalties."

## In plain words

In Singapore, a fixed sum payable on breach is enforceable if, when the contract was made, it was a genuine pre-estimate of the likely loss; if not, it is an unenforceable penalty. The rule applies only to sums triggered by a breach, so a fee payable on some other event (such as exercising an option to terminate) is not tested this way.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

Ask how a vendor's liquidated damages or early-termination sum was worked out. Under Singapore law a sum triggered by your breach that bears no relation to likely loss can be challenged, but a fee drafted as the price of an option rather than as a sum for breach may escape the rule. Contrast Malaysia, where s 75 and Cubic Electronics apply instead (my-case-cubic-electronics).
