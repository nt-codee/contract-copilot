## Text

Summary: a former CEO's termination agreement contained non-solicitation and non-competition clauses. The Court of Appeal restated the Singapore doctrine of restraint of trade, applying Lord Macnaghten's reasonableness test in Nordenfelt, and agreed with the trial judge that there was insufficient evidence of a legitimate proprietary interest behind the non-competition clause (Clause C.3), which was also far too wide in area.

At [79]: "There must always – and this is a fundamental legal proposition in this particular area of the law – be a legitimate proprietary interest which the court will then seek to protect by way of the doctrine of restraint of trade … However, even where a legitimate proprietary interest is shown, the court will ensure that the covenant in restraint of trade goes no further than what is necessary to protect the interest concerned."

At [80]: "In the context of the sale of a business, the main legitimate proprietary interest is that of goodwill."

## In plain words

In Singapore a non-compete or non-solicit is not automatically void, as it is in Malaysia, but it is enforceable only if it protects a real business interest (such as goodwill, trade secrets, trade connections) and goes no further than needed in scope, area and time. A bare ban on competing protects nothing and fails.

## When it matters in an NDA or service agreement

A non-compete inside an NDA usually adds nothing a confidentiality clause does not already protect, which makes it hard to justify under this test; the judgment also says that where confidential information is already covered by another clause, the restraint must protect something more. Ask for it to be removed, or narrowed to a specific product, area and short period.
