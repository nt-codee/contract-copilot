# Salary deductions and training bonds (employment)

clause_type: `salary_deductions` · contract_type: `employment`

## In plain words
When the employer may take money out of your pay, and whether you must repay training or other costs
if you leave early.

## What balanced looks like
- **Only lawful deductions**: tax, statutory contributions, and items you agreed to in writing.
- No deduction for damage, shortages or "losses" without your written consent for each case.
- **Training costs are not taken from wages.** If there is a bond, it covers only a real, costly
  course, is a **reasonable** amount, is **pro-rated** down over the bond period, and the cost is
  shown to you.

## Background (general, not advice)
- This is general information, not legal advice.
- In **Malaysia**, the Employment Act 1955 (s24) bans deductions from wages except those it allows,
  such as overpayments made in the last 3 months, wage advances, an indemnity where the employee
  leaves without notice, and items the employee asks for in writing. Recovering training costs is
  not on that list.
- In **Singapore**, courts have looked at training bonds; whether one holds depends on its terms.

## Red flags
- **High:** the employer may deduct training costs, fines or "losses" from your salary.
- **High:** a bond repayable in full however long you stayed, or far above the real course cost.
- **Medium:** a general consent to "any deductions the company considers appropriate".
- **Medium:** a bond for routine on-the-job training.
- **Low:** no breakdown of the cost a bond is based on.

## Suggested ask
"Please limit deductions to those allowed by law or agreed by me in writing each time, and make any
training bond a stated amount, pro-rated over the bond period and recovered only by agreement, not
from my wages."
