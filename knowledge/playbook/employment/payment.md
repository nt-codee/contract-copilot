# Payment for freelance and commission work (employment)

clause_type: `payment` · contract_type: `employment`

## In plain words
When and how you get paid for freelance work or commission: invoice timing, sign-off of your work,
late payment, and what happens if the work is cancelled.

## What balanced looks like
- **Payment within 14-30 days** of invoice or delivery.
- **Deemed acceptance:** the client has a review period of **7-14 days**; if it raises no specific
  defects in writing, the work counts as accepted.
- A deposit or milestone payments for longer projects.
- **Late interest** at a modest rate (for example around 1% a month) after a reminder.
- A **kill fee:** if the client cancels, it pays for work done plus a fair share of the remaining
  fee.
- Commission: a clear formula, when it is earned, and payment of earned commission after you leave.

## Background (general, not advice)
- In **Malaysia**, commission is excluded from "wages" when deciding the Employment Act 1955 salary
  threshold. A freelancer whose work is controlled by the client, uses its tools, and is paid
  regularly may in fact be treated as an employee (s101C).

## Red flags
- **High:** payment only after the client's own customer pays ("pay when paid"), or no date at all.
- **High:** cancellation at any time with nothing paid for work already done.
- **High:** payment due 90 days or more after a sign-off that the client controls (it may reject work or
  ask for unlimited changes, and nothing is deemed accepted), with no interest on late payment. The real
  wait then has no end.
- **Medium:** payment terms of 60-90 days.
- **Medium:** no review deadline, so the client can delay sign-off and payment indefinitely.
- **Low:** no late-payment interest.

## Suggested ask
"Please pay within 30 days of invoice, treat the work as accepted if no written defects are raised
within 10 days of delivery, and pay a kill fee for work done if the project is cancelled."
