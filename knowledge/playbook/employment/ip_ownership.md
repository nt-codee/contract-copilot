# Intellectual property ownership (employment)

clause_type: `ip_ownership` · contract_type: `employment`

## In plain words
Who owns the work you create: code, designs, writing, inventions. It also decides whether you keep
what you made before the job, or on your own time.

## What balanced looks like
- **Employees:** the employer owns work made **in the course of the job** or with its resources.
- You **keep pre-existing work** (list it in a schedule) and **unrelated personal projects** made on
  your own time and equipment.
- If pre-existing work is used in the job, the employer gets a licence to use it, not ownership.
- **Freelancers:** the client gets ownership (or a licence) of the **deliverables only, once paid in
  full**. You keep your pre-existing tools, templates, libraries and know-how, and give the client a
  licence to use them inside the deliverables.
- You may show the work in a portfolio unless it is confidential.

## Red flags
- **High:** the employer owns "all inventions and works" you create during employment, including on
  your own time and unrelated to the job.
- **High:** a freelancer's client owns everything on signing, before paying, including your existing
  tools.
- **Medium:** ownership covers work made for some period after you leave.
- **Medium:** you must waive all moral rights and give a power of attorney with no limit.
- **Low:** no schedule for listing pre-existing work.

## Suggested ask
Employee: "Please limit the assignment to work I create in the course of my duties, and confirm that I
keep my pre-existing work, tools and unrelated personal projects."
Freelancer or contractor: "Please limit the assignment to the deliverables once they are paid for, and
confirm that I keep my pre-existing work, tools and templates, with a licence to you to use any that are
built into the deliverables."
