# Termination (employment)

clause_type: `termination` · contract_type: `employment`

## In plain words
How the job can end: by notice without a reason, or at once for serious misconduct, and what you are
paid when it ends.

## What balanced looks like
- **Without cause:** either side may end the job by giving the agreed notice, or pay instead.
- **For cause:** dismissal without notice only for serious misconduct, after a fair process, with
  **written reasons**.
- **Pay owed on exit:** salary to the last day, unused annual leave, and any earned bonus or
  commission, paid promptly.
- No termination for being pregnant or on maternity leave.

## Background (general, not advice)
- In **Malaysia**, the Employment Act 1955 (s41A) bars an employer from ending a pregnant employee's
  job except for wilful breach, misconduct or closure of the business. Under the Industrial Relations
  Act 1967 (s20), a workman dismissed without just cause may complain in writing to the Director
  General **within 60 days**, and the case may go to the Industrial Court.
- In **Singapore**, the Employment Act sets baseline rules for many employees; the contract usually
  sets the notice terms.

## Red flags
- **High:** the employer may dismiss "at any time for any reason" without notice or pay.
- **High:** a clause allowing termination for pregnancy or maternity leave.
- **Medium:** "misconduct" defined so widely it covers minor issues, with no inquiry.
- **Medium:** earned commission or bonus forfeited on exit.
- **Low:** no timing for final pay.

## Suggested ask
"Please limit summary dismissal to serious misconduct with written reasons, and confirm that final
salary, unused leave and earned commission are paid within one pay cycle of my last day."
