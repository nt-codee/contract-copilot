# Non-solicitation of clients and staff (employment)

clause_type: `non_solicit` · contract_type: `employment`

## In plain words
A non-solicit stops you approaching your former employer's clients, or recruiting its staff, for a
period after you leave. It is narrower than a non-compete: you can still work in your field.

## What balanced looks like
- A short period: **6-12 months** after you leave.
- Limited to **clients you actually dealt with** in, say, your last 12 months, not every client the
  group has.
- Limited to **colleagues you worked with or managed**, and only active approaches: a public job
  advert or a colleague who contacts you first is not soliciting.
- No ban on clients choosing to follow you without being approached, or the clause says so clearly
  if it does apply.

## Background (general, not advice)
- In **Singapore**, client connections are a recognised legitimate interest, but a restraint wider
  than needed in time or scope is unenforceable. A ban on soliciting "any employee" was held
  unreasonable where the person had no influence over most staff (*3D Networks Singapore v Voon South
  Shiong* [2022] SGHC 167).
- In **Malaysia**, restraints on trade are void to the extent of the restraint under the Contracts
  Act 1950 (s28); how this applies to a non-solicit depends on its wording.

## Red flags
- **High:** 2 years or more, or covering all clients and prospects of the group worldwide.
- **Medium:** includes clients you never dealt with, or "potential clients".
- **Medium:** bans hiring any employee, even one who applies on their own.
- **Low:** no time limit stated for the staff part.

## Suggested ask
"Please limit the non-solicit to 6 months, and to clients and colleagues I personally dealt with in
my last 12 months of employment."
