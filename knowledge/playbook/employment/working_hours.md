# Working hours and overtime (employment)

clause_type: `working_hours` · contract_type: `employment`

## In plain words
How many hours you are expected to work, when, and what you get for working longer.

## What balanced looks like
- Normal hours stated clearly (days, start and end times, breaks), within legal norms.
- **Overtime is paid**, or given back as time off at an agreed rate, and only when asked for.
- Rest days and public holidays stated; work on them is paid at a higher rate or compensated.
- For senior or managerial staff not covered by overtime rules, hours stated as "reasonable" with
  some flexibility both ways.

## Background (general, not advice)
- In **Malaysia**, the Employment Act 1955 (s60A) sets normal limits of 8 hours a day and 45 hours
  a week, a 30-minute break after 5 straight hours, and overtime pay of at least 1.5 times the hourly
  rate. Since 1 Jan 2023, employees earning over RM4,000 a month are excluded from the overtime rule,
  unless they do manual labour or similar work (for example driving or supervising manual workers).
- In **Singapore**, the Employment Act sets baseline rules on hours and overtime for many employees.

## Red flags
- **High:** "overtime is deemed included in your salary" for a covered employee.
- **High:** normal hours above 45 a week in Malaysia, or no limit at all.
- **Medium:** "you must work such hours as the company requires" with no pay or time off.
- **Medium:** rest days or public holidays not mentioned.
- **Low:** hours given only as "office hours" with no times.

## Suggested ask
"Please state my normal hours and confirm that any overtime is paid at the statutory rate or
compensated with time off, rather than included in my salary."
