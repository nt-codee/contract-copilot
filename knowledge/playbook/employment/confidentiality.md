# Confidentiality (employment)

clause_type: `confidentiality` · contract_type: `employment`

## In plain words
What employer information you must keep secret, during the job and after it, and what you may still
use or disclose.

## What balanced looks like
- Covers genuinely confidential information: client lists, pricing, plans, source code, trade
  secrets.
- **Standard exclusions:** information that is public (not through your fault), that you already
  knew, that you got lawfully from someone else, or that a court or regulator requires you to
  disclose.
- Your **general skills, knowledge and experience** stay yours.
- **Ends or narrows** a few years (often 2-5) after you leave, except for trade secrets, which stay
  protected while they remain secret.
- A **whistleblowing carve-out:** nothing stops you reporting wrongdoing to a regulator, the police
  or a lawyer.
- Return or delete company materials when you leave.

## Background (general, not advice)
- In **Singapore**, trade secrets are a recognised legitimate interest an employer may protect
  (*Man Financial v Wong* [2007] SGCA 53).

## Red flags
- **High:** "all information" learned during employment is confidential forever, with no exclusions.
- **High:** bans you from reporting to regulators or authorities.
- **Medium:** treats your general skills and industry knowledge as company property.
- **Medium:** a penalty or fixed damages for any breach, however small.
- **Low:** no rule on returning or deleting materials.

## Suggested ask
"Please add the standard exclusions and a carve-out for reports to regulators, protect my general
skills and knowledge, and limit the obligation to 3 years after I leave except for trade secrets."
