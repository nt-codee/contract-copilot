# Notice period (employment)

clause_type: `notice_period` · contract_type: `employment`

## In plain words
How much warning you or your employer must give to end the job, and whether pay can be given
instead of notice.

## What balanced looks like
- **The same notice length for both sides.**
- A length that suits the role: often 1 month for junior staff, 2-3 months for senior staff.
- Notice in writing; either side may pay salary instead of working the notice.
- One notice clause that matches the rest of the contract (probation notice can be shorter, but
  stated).

## Background (general, not advice)
- In **Malaysia**, the Employment Act 1955 (s12) says notice "shall be the same for both employer
  and employee" and must be in writing. If the contract says nothing, the minimum is 4 weeks (under 2
  years' service), 6 weeks (2 to under 5 years) or 8 weeks (5 years and more).
- In **Singapore**, the Employment Act sets baseline notice rules for many employees; the contract
  usually sets the length.

## Red flags
- **High:** you must give 3 months' notice while the employer may end the job on 1 week.
- **Medium:** notice periods that differ between clauses (for example the letter says 1 month, the
  handbook 3 months).
- **Medium:** the employer may refuse pay instead of notice for you but use it for itself.
- **Low:** no mention of written notice or the start date of the notice period.

## Suggested ask
Employee: "Please make the notice period the same for both sides, for example 1 month in writing, with
either side able to pay salary in lieu of notice."
Freelancer or contractor (paid fees, not a salary): "Please make the notice period the same for both
sides, for example 30 days in writing, and pay for all work done up to the end of the notice period."
