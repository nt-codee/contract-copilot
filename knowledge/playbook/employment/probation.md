# Probation (employment)

clause_type: `probation` · contract_type: `employment`

## In plain words
Probation is a trial period at the start of a job. During it the employer checks whether you fit the
role, and the contract often allows either side to end the job on shorter notice.

## What balanced looks like
- **3 months is typical**; up to **6 months** for senior or specialist roles.
- A fixed start and end date, and a written confirmation (or not) at the end.
- **Extension at most once**, in writing, with the reasons given and a new fixed end date.
- The **same pay and benefits** as a confirmed employee, apart from items that clearly start on
  confirmation.
- Notice during probation is **the same length for both sides** (often 1-2 weeks).

## Background (general, not advice)
- In **Malaysia**, the Employment Act 1955 has no probation rules; probation is a contract term, and
  notice during probation follows what the contract says. A probationer is still an employee.
- In **Singapore**, probation is also a matter for the contract and market practice.

## Red flags
- **High:** the employer may extend probation again and again, or "until further notice".
- **Medium:** probation longer than 6 months for an ordinary role.
- **Medium:** the employer may end the job without notice during probation, while you must give weeks.
- **Medium:** lower pay or no leave during probation without a clear reason.
- **Low:** no date or process for written confirmation.

## Suggested ask
"Please set probation at 3 months, allow one extension of up to 3 months only with written reasons
and a fixed end date, and give both sides the same notice period during probation."
