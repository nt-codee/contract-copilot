# Rent increases and rent review (tenancy)

clause_type: `rent_review` · contract_type: `tenancy`

## In plain words
Whether the rent can change during the term and who decides. Rent reviews matter most in longer terms and in renewals.

## What balanced looks like
- **Fixed rent for the initial term** for a home, with any increase only on renewal and agreed in writing.
- For a longer shop-lot term, a **scheduled step-up** (for example a stated percentage at the start of year 2 and 3) or a **review to open-market rent** with a cap, such as a rise of no more than **10% to 15%** at each review, or a collar and cap.
- At least **2 to 3 months' written notice** of any new rent, with a right for the tenant to decline and leave.
- A review based on **objective evidence** (a named index, comparable rents, or an independent valuer), not just on one party's opinion.
- The tenant is told the review date in advance.

## Red flags
- **High:** A rent increase **at the landlord's sole discretion** or 'as the landlord may determine', with no cap, no notice period and no way out.
- **High:** Automatic rent increases of **more than 10% a year**, or an increase that compounds on renewal.
- **Medium:** The new rent is set by the landlord's own valuer only.
- **Medium:** A review clause that only works one way (rent can go up but never down), where the market could fall.
- **Low:** A fixed step-up that is clear, capped and diarised.

## Suggested ask
"Please replace the landlord's discretion with a fixed schedule or a review to open-market rent capped at 10% a review, with 3 months' written notice and the tenant's right to leave if it does not accept the new rent."
