# Insurance and liability for the premises (tenancy)

clause_type: `insurance` · contract_type: `tenancy`

## In plain words
Who insures the building and contents, and who is responsible if something goes wrong. In tenancies the landlord normally insures the building and the tenant insures its own contents and, for a shop, its liability to the public.

## What balanced looks like
- The **landlord** insures the **building** (fire and similar risks); the **tenant** insures its **own contents, stock and fit-out**.
- A shop-lot tenant carries **public liability** insurance at a stated, reasonable amount, and shows the certificate on request.
- Each side is responsible for **its own negligence**; the tenant is not made to cover the landlord's negligence or events outside its control.
- If the premises are damaged by fire or other insured events, **rent is suspended** and either side may end the tenancy if repairs take too long.
- Insurance premiums that the landlord passes on are **reasonable, itemised** and relate to the building.

## Red flags
- **High:** The tenant is liable for **all loss or damage to the building**, including from causes outside its control, or must indemnify the landlord for the landlord's own negligence.
- **High:** Rent continues in full after the premises are destroyed or unusable.
- **Medium:** The tenant must buy insurance in the landlord's chosen company, at an unspecified sum.
- **Medium:** The landlord's insurance cost is passed on in an unlimited amount with no breakdown.
- **Low:** A requirement to show the tenant's insurance certificate once a year.

## Suggested ask
"Please limit the tenant's insurance to its own contents and public liability at a stated amount, and add that rent stops if the premises are unusable because of fire or other insured damage."
