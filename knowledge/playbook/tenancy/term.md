# Tenancy term and start date (tenancy)

clause_type: `term` · contract_type: `tenancy`

## In plain words
How long the tenancy lasts, when it starts, and whether it is fixed or rolling. The term decides how long each side is committed and, for rent and deposit, how much is at stake.

## What balanced looks like
- A **fixed term stated in months** with clear start and end dates, commonly **12 months** for a home and **2 to 3 years** for a shop lot.
- Shop lots often have a longer first term (**3 years**) because the tenant pays for fit-out; a lease **over 3 years** in Peninsular Malaysia is a registered lease, so ask whether the parties intend one.
- A **handover date** and the condition at handover are recorded, for example with a signed checklist and photos.
- A **rent-free fit-out period** for a shop lot where the tenant has to renovate.
- The **stamp duty** is dealt with: who arranges it, who pays, and by when (Malaysia: within 30 days of signing; Singapore: via IRAS e-stamping).

## Red flags
- **High:** The term is unclear or inconsistent (for example '12 months' in one clause and '2 years' in another), or no end date is given.
- **High:** The tenant must pay a large fit-out or premium sum up front but the term is short and there is no option to renew or compensation if the landlord ends early.
- **Medium:** No handover checklist, so the tenant can later be charged for existing damage.
- **Medium:** The agreement is not stamped, or says nothing about stamping, so it may not be usable in court until duty and a late-stamping penalty are paid.
- **Low:** A fixed term without a date for the tenant to give notice of renewal; usually fixable by asking for a diary date.

## Suggested ask
"Please state the start date, end date and handover condition (with a signed checklist), and confirm who will stamp the agreement and who pays the duty."
