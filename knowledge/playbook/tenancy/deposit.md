# Security deposit and refund (tenancy)

clause_type: `deposit` · contract_type: `tenancy`

## In plain words
The deposit is money the tenant pays at the start as security for rent, utilities and damage. A fair clause says how much it is, what it can be used for, when it is returned and how deductions are proved.

## What balanced looks like
- **Residential:** about **2 months' rent** as a security deposit, plus roughly **half a month** for utilities, in Malaysia; in Singapore a deposit of **1 month's rent per year of lease, up to 2 months**, is common. **Shop lots:** **2 to 3 months'** rent is common; 3 months is usual for a new shop-lot tenant.
- Returned **within 14 to 30 days** after the tenant gives back the keys and the premises are checked.
- Deductions only for **unpaid rent, unpaid utilities and damage beyond fair wear and tear**, shown by receipts or a written list.
- The tenant may not treat the deposit as the last month's rent, and the landlord may not use it for an ordinary rent delay without notice.
- If the landlord sells the property, the deposit passes to the buyer or is refunded.

## Red flags
- **High:** A deposit that is **non-refundable**, or refundable only **'at the landlord's discretion'**, or forfeited in full for any breach, regardless of the landlord's actual loss.
- **High:** Forfeiture of the **whole deposit** if the tenant leaves early or gives notice, with no link to the landlord's loss or to the time it takes to re-let.
- **High:** A deposit of **3 months or more for a home**, or more than 3 months for a shop lot, with no interest or protection.
- **Medium:** No deadline for returning the deposit, or the landlord may deduct for 'cleaning', 'painting' or 'wear and tear' with no proof.
- **Medium:** Deposits are described as 'earnest' or a 'penalty' in one clause and as security in another.
- **Low:** A utility deposit that is returned only after the final bills arrive; reasonable if there is a long-stop date.

## Suggested ask
"Please make the security deposit refundable within 21 days of handover back, with deductions limited to unpaid rent and utilities and damage beyond fair wear and tear, each shown by a written list or receipts."
