# Repairs, maintenance and structural items (tenancy)

clause_type: `repairs` · contract_type: `tenancy`

## In plain words
Who pays for and carries out repairs. In general the landlord looks after the structure and major systems, and the tenant looks after day-to-day wear and small fixes. A fair clause draws that line clearly.

## What balanced looks like
- **Landlord:** the **structure** (roof, external walls, foundations, main drains), **major systems** (main electrical supply, plumbing in walls, lifts, air-conditioning plant, fire safety) and **defects that existed at handover**.
- **Tenant:** **day-to-day upkeep and minor repairs** (light bulbs, taps, filters, minor fittings), and **damage caused by the tenant or its visitors**.
- For a shop lot, the tenant pays for its own **fit-out**, signage and shop-front maintenance, but not for **structural repairs** or the building's base services.
- The landlord must repair within a **reasonable time** after notice, and the tenant may arrange urgent repairs and be reimbursed.
- The landlord gets **reasonable access** on notice (usually 24 to 48 hours) for inspections and repairs.
- Rent is **reduced** if the premises cannot be used because of a landlord repair.

## Red flags
- **High:** The tenant pays **all repairs, including structural repairs** and the roof, external walls or main services, or pays a share of the landlord's capital works.
- **High:** The tenant must hand back the premises in **'as new' condition**, or in better condition than at handover, regardless of fair wear and tear.
- **Medium:** No handover condition record, so the tenant cannot show what was already damaged.
- **Medium:** The landlord may enter at any time without notice.
- **Low:** The tenant does minor repairs up to a stated small amount; reasonable if the amount is fixed.

## Suggested ask
"Please limit the tenant's repair duty to day-to-day upkeep and damage the tenant causes; the landlord keeps the structure and the main services, with repairs done within 14 days of notice."
