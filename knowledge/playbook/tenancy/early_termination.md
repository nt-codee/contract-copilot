# Early exit by the tenant (tenancy)

clause_type: `early_termination` · contract_type: `tenancy`

## In plain words
What happens if the tenant wants to leave before the fixed term ends: notice period, fees, and what the landlord can keep. Fair terms let the tenant leave in return for a defined, proportionate cost.

## What balanced looks like
- A **break clause** for a fixed term of 12 months or more: the tenant can leave after a stated period (often **6 to 12 months**) on **2 months' written notice**.
- The cost of leaving is **proportionate**: for example **1 to 2 months' rent**, or the landlord's real loss until it re-lets, whichever is less, and the landlord must try to **re-let**.
- If the tenant leaves early, the **deposit is returned** minus the agreed early-exit amount and any proven damage or arrears.
- For homes, a **diplomatic clause** (a right to leave early if the tenant is posted out of the country) or a right to propose a replacement tenant.
- A pro-rata refund of **rent paid in advance**.

## Red flags
- **High:** **Forfeiture of the entire deposit** for early exit, with no link to the landlord's loss or to re-letting.
- **High:** The tenant owes **all rent for the rest of the term** if it leaves, even if the landlord re-lets.
- **Medium:** A break fee of **more than 3 months' rent**, or one that applies even after a break date.
- **Medium:** No right to find a replacement tenant.
- **Low:** A modest administrative fee for leaving early, clearly stated.

## Suggested ask
"Please let the tenant leave on 2 months' written notice after month 6, in return for a fee of one month's rent and the return of the balance of the deposit, with the landlord to try to re-let."
