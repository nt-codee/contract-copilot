# Default and repossession (hire purchase)

clause_type: `repossession` · contract_type: `hire_purchase`

## In plain words
What happens if you miss payments, and when the lender may take the car back.

## What balanced looks like
- **A written warning before repossession.** In Malaysia the Hire-Purchase Act requires two successive missed payments and a Fourth Schedule notice with at least 21 days to fix it. Once more than 75% of the cash price is paid the owner also needs a court order.
- **A chance to remedy**: pay the arrears and the agreement carries on.
- **Return within the notice period** without repossession or storage costs (Malaysia, s 16A).
- **A notice after repossession** to you and any guarantor, and a holding period before the car is sold (Malaysia: 21 days).
- **Only licensed or permitted recovery agents**, no entering your home or taking the car by force.
- **Singapore:** the Hire-Purchase Act 1969 requires notices and, once a set share of the price has been paid, a court order before the owner can repossess. Check the Act for the threshold.

## Red flags
- **High:** repossession 'without notice' or after a single missed instalment.
- **High:** the lender may enter premises or take the car without following the statutory steps (void in Malaysia under s 34(e)).
- **High:** repossession on bankruptcy or on any other event unrelated to payment, with no notice.
- **Medium:** the lender may sell the car at once and keep the whole price.
- **Medium:** you are liable for 'all repossession costs' even if you hand the car back on time.
- **Low:** notice is by SMS or email only, with no fixed address for post.

## Suggested ask
"Please state that the lender will not repossess without the statutory notice, that I may remedy the arrears within the notice period, and that I am not liable for repossession costs if I return the goods within that period."
