# Term: how long the NDA and the secrecy duty last (NDA)

clause_type: `term` · contract_type: `nda`

## In plain words
Two different clocks. The **agreement term** is the window during which information may be shared
under the NDA. The **confidentiality period** is how long the receiver must keep it secret after
that.

## What balanced looks like
- Agreement term of **1 to 2 years**, or until the deal is signed or abandoned.
- Confidentiality period of **2 to 3 years** after disclosure or after the NDA ends. Two years is the
  most common fixed period in published market surveys.
- **Up to 5 years** can be reasonable for very sensitive material, but it should be a deliberate
  choice, not a default.
- **Trade secrets** (for example source code or a secret recipe) may stay protected for as long as
  they remain secret. That is a narrow, reasonable exception.
- The same periods apply to both sides.

## Red flags
- **High:** confidentiality that lasts "in perpetuity", "indefinitely" or "forever" for **all**
  information, not only trade secrets. The receiver then carries the risk of breach for ever.
- **Medium:** the secrecy duty covers only information disclosed in a short window (for example the first
  year) or ends after 1-2 years even for trade secrets and business-critical information, and the
  receiver may then use or pass it on freely.
- **Medium:** a 5-year or longer period for ordinary business information, especially when only one
  side is bound.
- **Medium:** the clock starts only when the discloser says so, or restarts every time new
  information is shared.
- **Low:** no stated term at all; ask for one so everyone knows when duties end.

## Suggested ask
"Please set the confidentiality period to two (or at most three) years after disclosure, with
longer protection only for genuine trade secrets."
