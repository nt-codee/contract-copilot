# Governing law: which country's law applies (NDA)

clause_type: `governing_law` · contract_type: `nda`

## In plain words
The governing law decides which country's rules are used to read the contract. The jurisdiction
wording decides where a court case would be heard. Together they decide how expensive and
unfamiliar any dispute would be.

## What balanced looks like
- For two Malaysian companies: **Malaysian law**, courts in Malaysia (often Kuala Lumpur).
- For a Malaysian and a Singaporean company: **Malaysian or Singapore law**, with the matching
  courts. Both are common, familiar and close to home.
- Either side may still seek an urgent injunction in any court where the other side is.
- Non-exclusive jurisdiction is friendlier than exclusive jurisdiction in a far-away court.

## Red flags
- **High:** the law and **exclusive** courts of a distant country (for example a US state or England)
  when neither party's business is based there. A small business would need foreign lawyers, and
  travel, even to defend a weak claim.
- **High:** the discloser's home law and **exclusive** courts in a distant country (outside Asia-Pacific,
  for example a US state or England) together with a waiver of any objection to that forum. A small
  receiver cannot realistically defend or enforce a claim there, so the clause works as a threat.
- **Medium:** the discloser's home law when the receiver is the smaller party, with exclusive courts
  there, in a nearby country; workable between Malaysia and Singapore.
- **Medium:** the law of one place but the courts of another, which multiplies cost.
- **Low:** no governing law stated; ask for one to avoid an argument later.

## Suggested ask
"Please change the governing law to Malaysian (or Singapore) law, with the courts of Kuala Lumpur
(or Singapore), since both parties do business in this region."
