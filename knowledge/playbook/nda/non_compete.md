# Non-compete inside an NDA

clause_type: `non_compete` · contract_type: `nda`

## In plain words
A non-compete stops a business from working in a market, or with competitors, for a period. It is a
much bigger promise than keeping information secret.

## What balanced looks like
- **A standard NDA has no non-compete at all.** It protects information, not market position.
- Many standard forms say the opposite: the receiver remains free to build or buy similar products,
  as long as it does not misuse the confidential information.
- Where a business restriction is genuinely needed (for example in an exclusive distribution deal),
  it belongs in the main commercial agreement, negotiated openly, short, and limited to a specific
  product and territory.

## Background (general, not advice)
- In **Malaysia**, section 28 of the Contracts Act 1950 says an agreement that restrains someone from
  carrying on a lawful business is void, with narrow exceptions such as the sale of a business's
  goodwill.
- In **Singapore**, a restraint is enforceable only if it protects a legitimate interest and is
  reasonable in scope, area and time.
- Even a restraint that may not hold up in court can deter a small business, cost money to fight, or
  be enforceable under a foreign governing law.

## Red flags
- **High:** any clause that stops the receiver from doing business in a sector, region or with named
  companies, especially under a vague heading such as "Protection of Business Interests".
- **High:** a restriction lasting years after the NDA, or across a whole region (for example "Southeast
  Asia").
- **Medium:** a promise not to deal with the discloser's customers or suppliers.

## Suggested ask
"Please delete the non-compete wording; this NDA should only protect confidential information, and
either party should stay free to do business as long as it does not misuse that information."
