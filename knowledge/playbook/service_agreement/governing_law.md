# Governing law: which country's law applies (service agreement)

clause_type: `governing_law` · contract_type: `service_agreement`

## In plain words
Which country's law is used to read the contract, and where a case would be heard. A distant law or
court makes any dispute slower and more expensive for the smaller side.

## What balanced looks like
- The law of the country where the **customer** receives the service, or where both sides are based.
- Malaysian law with Kuala Lumpur courts, or Singapore law with Singapore courts, for a contract
  between companies in these two countries.
- The courts named match the law chosen.
- Non-exclusive jurisdiction, or arbitration in the region (see `dispute_resolution.md`).

## Red flags
- **High:** the law and exclusive courts of a country where neither party operates.
- **High:** a foreign law and exclusive foreign courts although the service is performed entirely in the
  customer's own country, especially when the customer must bear its own travel and legal costs. Suing
  over a local delivery in a distant court is not realistic for an SME.
- **Medium:** the vendor's home law and exclusive courts when the vendor is much larger and abroad, but
  the service is not delivered in the customer's country, or the courts are nearby.
- **Medium:** a mismatch: one country's law but another country's courts.
- **Low:** no governing law clause.

## Suggested ask
"Please make the agreement subject to Malaysian law (or Singapore law) and the courts of Kuala
Lumpur (or Singapore), where both parties operate."
