# Indemnity: promising to cover the other side's losses (service agreement)

clause_type: `indemnity` · contract_type: `service_agreement`

## In plain words
An indemnity is a promise to pay the other side's losses and legal costs if a particular thing
happens, usually a claim brought by an outsider. It can be much wider than ordinary damages, so its
scope matters.

## What balanced looks like
- **Vendor indemnifies the customer** if a third party claims the vendor's service or deliverables
  infringe their IP (copyright, patent, trade mark). This is the most common indemnity.
- **Customer indemnifies the vendor** only if a third party claims that content or data the customer
  supplied infringes their rights or breaks the law.
- Indemnities cover **third-party claims**, not every loss between the two parties.
- The protected side must **tell the other promptly**, let it **control the defence**, and cooperate.
- Losses caused by the protected side's own negligence or breach are excluded.
- Indemnity amounts are capped like other liability, or sit under an agreed higher cap.

## Red flags
- **High:** the customer indemnifies the vendor against "any and all" losses "arising out of or in
  connection with" the agreement, even those caused by the vendor's own negligence.
- **High:** a one-way indemnity from the customer only, with no IP indemnity from the vendor.
- **Medium:** the indemnity covers the vendor's own losses (not only third-party claims) and is
  uncapped.
- **Medium:** no duty to notify, and no right for the indemnifying side to control the defence.

## Suggested ask
"Please limit the customer's indemnity to third-party claims caused by our own data or breach,
exclude losses caused by the vendor's negligence, and add the vendor's standard IP infringement
indemnity."
