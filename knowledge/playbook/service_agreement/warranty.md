# Warranties: what the vendor promises about the service (service agreement)

clause_type: `warranty` · contract_type: `service_agreement`

## In plain words
A warranty is a promise about quality: that the service will be done properly and the software will
work as described. It also says what the customer gets if the promise is broken.

## What balanced looks like
- Services performed **with reasonable skill and care**, by suitably qualified people, in line with
  good industry practice.
- Deliverables and software **match the agreed specification** or documentation.
- **A defects period** of about **90 days** after acceptance in which the vendor fixes defects free.
- **Service levels** for hosted services (for example 99.5% monthly availability) with service
  credits if missed, and a right to end the contract after repeated failures.
- Vendor confirms it has the rights to provide the service and complies with applicable law.
- Disclaimers of other implied promises are acceptable once the above are given.

## Red flags
- **High:** everything provided "as is" with no promise of any kind, for a paid service.
- **Medium:** the only remedy is a small service credit, even for a long outage.
- **Medium:** defects must be reported within a few days, or the warranty is lost.
- **Medium:** no acceptance testing before the customer must pay in full.
- **Low:** a 30-day defects period instead of 90; worth asking to extend.

## Suggested ask
"Please add a warranty that services are performed with reasonable skill and care and match the
specification, with free fixes for defects reported within 90 days of acceptance."
