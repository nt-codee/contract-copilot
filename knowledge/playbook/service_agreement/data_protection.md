# Personal data protection (service agreement)

clause_type: `data_protection` · contract_type: `service_agreement`

## In plain words
When a vendor handles personal data (about the customer's staff, patients or customers) on the
customer's behalf, the contract should say how the vendor keeps it safe. Malaysia and Singapore each
have a Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA), and in both the customer usually stays responsible for
what its vendors do with the data.

## What balanced looks like
- The vendor processes personal data **only on the customer's instructions** and only to provide the
  service.
- **Reasonable security** measures, described or referenced (access control, encryption, staff
  training).
- **Breach notice** to the customer **without undue delay**, with a fixed outer limit such as 24-72
  hours after discovery, so the customer can meet its own reporting duties.
- **Subcontractors** only with notice to the customer and on the same terms.
- **Transfers outside Malaysia or Singapore** only with adequate protection.
- **Return or deletion** of personal data at the end, with written confirmation.
- Help with requests from individuals to access or correct their data.
- Liability for data breaches is not excluded; often under a higher, mutual "super-cap".

## Red flags
- **High:** no data protection clause at all when the service handles personal data.
- **High:** the vendor may use the personal data for its own purposes (marketing, training products,
  selling insights).
- **Medium:** breach notice only "as soon as practicable" with no outer limit, or none at all.
- **Medium:** data may be stored or transferred anywhere without notice.
- **Low:** security described only in general terms; ask for a summary of measures.

## Suggested ask
"Please add a data protection clause: processing only on our instructions, reasonable security,
breach notice within 48 hours, approved subcontractors, and return or deletion of data at the end."
