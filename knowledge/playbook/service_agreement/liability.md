# Limitation of liability: the most either side can be made to pay (service agreement)

clause_type: `liability` · contract_type: `service_agreement`

## In plain words
A liability cap sets the maximum amount one side can be made to pay the other if things go wrong.
An exclusion lists kinds of loss that cannot be claimed at all, such as lost profits.

## What balanced looks like
- **Mutual cap**: the same cap for both sides, usually **the fees paid or payable in the 12 months**
  before the claim.
- **Mutual exclusion** of indirect losses (lost profits, lost business, loss of reputation) for both
  sides.
- **Carve-outs from the cap** (amounts that stay uncapped or have a higher cap) apply to both sides
  equally: fraud, death or personal injury caused by negligence, breach of confidentiality, data
  protection breaches (often a higher "super-cap", such as 2-3 times annual fees), and the vendor's
  IP infringement indemnity.
- The customer's obligation to pay fees for services received is not limited by the cap.

## Red flags
- **High:** the vendor's cap is tiny, for example **one month's fees** or a fixed small sum, so even
  a serious failure costs the vendor almost nothing.
- **High:** the customer's liability is **uncapped** while the vendor's is capped: a one-sided cap.
- **High:** the vendor excludes liability for data loss or security breaches entirely.
- **Medium:** a cap of 3-6 months' fees, or a cap that applies only to the vendor's side.
- **Medium:** the indirect-loss exclusion protects only the vendor.
- **Low:** a 12-month mutual cap with a lower carve-out list; usually acceptable.

## Suggested ask
"Please make the limitation of liability mutual, set the cap at the fees paid in the previous 12
months for both parties, and apply the same exclusions and carve-outs to both."
