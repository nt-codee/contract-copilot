# Payment, late interest and price increases (service agreement)

clause_type: `payment` · contract_type: `service_agreement`

## In plain words
How much is paid, when, what happens if payment is late, and whether the vendor may raise prices.

## What balanced looks like
- **Payment within 30 days** of a correct invoice (30-60 days is common in Malaysia and Singapore).
- Prices stated **excluding tax**, with Malaysian service tax (SST) or Singapore GST added where it
  applies.
- **Disputed invoices:** the customer may hold back the disputed part in good faith while paying the
  rest, and both sides try to resolve it quickly.
- **Late interest** at a modest rate, roughly **0.5% to 1% a month** (or a fixed annual rate around
  5-8%), only after a reminder.
- **Suspension** for non-payment only after written notice and a further 14-30 days.
- **Price increases** no more than once a year, with **at least 60 days' notice**, capped at
  inflation or a fixed low figure (for example 3-5%), and a right to leave if the customer refuses.

## Red flags
- **High:** an automatic increase of **10% or more every year**, or an increase "at the vendor's
  discretion" with no cap.
- **Medium:** payment within 7 or 14 days.
- **Medium:** late interest of 2% a month or more, or interest plus collection fees plus suspension.
- **Medium:** the customer must pay in full even when it disputes the invoice.
- **Low:** fees payable annually in advance; common for software, but check refund terms.

## Suggested ask
"Please set payment at 30 days from invoice, late interest at no more than 1% a month, and cap
any annual price increase at 5% with 60 days' written notice."
